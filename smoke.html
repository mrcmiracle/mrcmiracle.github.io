Wildfire smoke

Smoke season in King County generally runs through late summer and early fall. The particles in smoke are small enough to reach deep into your lungs.

Make one room breathable

You do not need to clean your whole home. Pick the room with the fewest windows and doors, close it off, and run an air cleaner there. This is where you sleep and spend the day when the air is bad. A box fan taped to a MERV-13 furnace filter works and costs far less than a purifier. Build it before smoke season starts.

Keep outside air out

Close windows and doors. Set central heat or AC to recirculate. Close the fresh-air vent on a window unit. Use a damp towel along a drafty door. Do not run a whole-house or attic fan, and do not use a swamp cooler, since both pull outside air in.

Stop adding particles indoors

Vacuuming, candles, incense, frying, broiling, gas stoves, and smoking all add particles to air you are trying to clean. Skip them while smoke lasts. Use a microwave or an electric kettle instead of the stovetop.

Masks

An N95 or KN95 that seals against your face filters smoke particles. Cloth masks, surgical masks, and bandanas do not. Facial hair breaks the seal. Masks do not fit small children, so keep them indoors instead.

Who needs to be more careful

People with asthma, COPD, or heart disease, people who are pregnant, young children, and older adults feel effects sooner and more severely. If that is you or someone in your home, act at lower smoke levels rather than waiting to feel bad. Keep rescue medication where you can reach it.

When to get help

Trouble breathing, chest pain, a fast heartbeat, dizziness, or confusion means get medical help right away. Call 911 for anything life-threatening.
